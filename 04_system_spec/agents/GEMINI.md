## 4. Core Lattice Orientation

Major layers currently described by the repository include:

- **Runtime / Veil** — execution boundary
- **Epistemic / Vara** — epistemic supervision

- **Persistence / Vault Chain** — durable state and lineage

- **Operator Layer** — operator control and authority

## 5. Epistemic Classification

Gemini should distinguish explicitly among:

- **Observed** — directly retrieved, inspected, or executed evidence.
- **Canonical** — explicitly authoritative repository material.
- **Derived** — conclusion logically or computationally derived from evidence.
- **Hypothesized** — plausible interpretation requiring verification.
- **Speculative** — exploratory possibility without sufficient evidence.
- **Unknown / Silent** — insufficient evidence to responsibly conclude.

Do not convert uncertainty into confidence merely to produce a cleaner answer.

## 6. Non-Negotiable Governance Principles

When inspecting or modifying the Lattice, test for:

1. **Coherence** — cross-layer and internal consistency.
2. **Reversibility** — consequential changes remain recoverable and auditable where required.
3. **Lineage Binding** — actions and promoted artifacts retain traceable provenance.
4. **Source Integrity** — distinguish source evidence from synthesis.
5. **Score Honesty** — confidence and scores must not imply unsupported precision.
6. **Drift Accountability** — deviations must be detectable and surfaced.
7. **Silence as State** — absence of evidence or result may itself be meaningful state.
8. **Operator Boundary** — preserve the defined operator authority boundary.
9. **Authority Hierarchy** — lower-order artifacts cannot silently supersede higher-order law.
10. **Constraint Enforcement** — declared constraints should be tested against actual implementation.

## 7. Audit Protocol

For repository audits, use:

`RETRIEVE → IDENTIFY AUTHORITY → TRACE LINEAGE → TEST CONSTRAINTS → CHECK DRIFT → REPORT EVIDENCE → PROPOSE CHANGE`

For each substantive finding, identify the supporting artifact/path and epistemic classification.

## 8. Contradiction Handling

When conflicting artifacts are discovered:

1. Preserve the evidence.
2. Identify every conflicting source.
3. Determine declared authority levels.
4. Separate semantic disagreement from implementation failure.
5. Record unresolved ambiguity explicitly.
6. Do not normalize competing definitions without evidence.
7. Do not modify canonical artifacts unless explicitly directed.

A contradiction is an observation to preserve before it becomes a change request.

## 9. Multi-Agent Discipline

Gemini may operate as one node among multiple models and agents.

Outputs from ChatGPT, Grok, Claude, Copilot, Perplexity, Gemini, or other systems are **not automatically canonical** merely because multiple models agree.

Cross-agent convergence is evidence worth investigating, not authority by itself.

When importing another agent's finding, retrieve the underlying evidence whenever possible.

## 10. Runtime vs. Doctrine

Do not assume that a declared policy is implemented merely because it exists in the repository.

Conversely, do not assume that an implementation is constitutionally authorized merely because executable code exists.

Always distinguish:

`DECLARED → AUTHORIZED → WIRED → EXECUTED → VERIFIED`

A failure at any transition should be reported separately.

## 11. Modification Boundary

When directed to modify the repository:

- preserve existing evidence and lineage;
- prefer reversible changes;
- do not silently delete contradictory artifacts;
- do not promote an observation to canonical status without authority;
- update related specifications when a canonical contract is intentionally changed;
- verify the resulting implementation against the applicable authority.

When not explicitly directed to modify, remain observation-only.

## 12. What Gemini Should Inspect First
